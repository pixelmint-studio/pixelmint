DOOMSOL: Web3 gaming on Solana

Web3 Gaming / Solana / Game LaunchLaunched August 2026

DOOMSOL explored the intersection of classic gaming and Web3 through a browser-based first-person shooter connected to the Solana ecosystem. Launched in August 2026, it brought accessible gameplay, competitive scoring, cryptocurrency rewards and a token launch together in one gaming initiative—bridging traditional game mechanics and crypto-community participation.

Pixelmint helped develop DOOMSOL. The project is not presented as an official DOOM product or as endorsed or licensed by Bethesda or id Software.

The Gaming Experience

Players could try the browser-based classic DOOM gameplay without buying a token or connecting a wallet just to play.

  • Browser gameplay accessible on desktop.
  • Competitive scoring for kills, collected items, discovered secrets and completed levels.
  • Leaderboards based on players' best qualifying runs.
  • SOL rewards for top-performing players; wallet use was optional for play, with a wallet used by players receiving cryptocurrency rewards.

The Web3 Component

A Solana-based token, $DOOM, launched through pump.fun alongside the gaming experience. The project connected:

  • A playable game intended to attract users.
  • A competitive reward mechanism using SOL.
  • A crypto-native community and token launch.

This model shows how blockchain incentives can sit alongside a game without making token ownership a prerequisite for participation.

Launch and Market Exposure

The project launched in early August 2026 and drew substantial initial attention in crypto communities. Contemporary reporting put the token's brief peak market capitalization at approximately $7–8 million.

This is a reported peak market capitalization—not company revenue, funds raised or realized investor returns. The token later declined substantially, underscoring the volatility of speculative launches and the difference between initial attention and sustained product adoption.

Key Takeaways

  • Gameplay should provide value independently of token speculation.
  • Reward mechanisms need to be clear, reliable and sustainable.
  • Community engagement can accelerate awareness during a launch.
  • Token performance is not the same as product-market fit.
  • Rights and licensing must be addressed before commercializing games based on established franchises.

Discuss a Project

Questions creators ask

Do I need to know anything about crypto?

No. We handle the technical and crypto side entirely. You decide how your game and characters are represented.

Do I have to promote the coin?

No. Your involvement is optional. If you would like to post about it, design a level or join a live session with fans, we agree on it together and any promotion is clearly disclosed as a partnership.

Who owns my game and characters?

You do, always. The agreement gives us a limited license to use the assets you approve, for this project only.

How and when am I paid?

You receive a percentage of the launch revenue in a single payout once launch revenues are collected. The exact percentage, what counts as revenue and the payment date are all written into the agreement before anything starts.

Is the coin an investment?

No. Each coin is a community token that gives holders access to fan perks such as exclusive art, merch drops and in-game events. We never promise prices or returns.

What happens if I say no?

Nothing. We never launch anything using a game without a signed license from its owner.
